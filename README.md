# Regulating the Feed

**A comparative empirical legal study of enacted social media regulation across eight selected regulatory systems**

*Regulating the Feed* examines how enacted laws and regulations are beginning to govern the design and operation of social media platforms directly, including account access, feeds and recommendation systems, and engagement features.

The project uses a systematically coded dataset of **34 regulatory actions** identified across seven of the eight selected regulatory systems. Canada remained part of the comparative design but produced no qualifying action during the study period. The study compares the emerging U.S. state landscape with regulatory approaches outside the United States and separately tracks whether enacted requirements were legally operative as of **September 11, 2026**.

## Research Question

> How are selected regulatory systems using enacted law to regulate social media access, recommendation systems, and engagement design, and how does the approach emerging among U.S. states compare with approaches in the other sampled systems?

## Study Design

The project is a **descriptive comparative empirical legal study** based on a purposive sample of eight regulatory systems.

Legal requirements were coded across three nonexclusive **areas of regulation**:

- **Account and Service Access**
- **Feeds and Recommendation Systems**
- **Engagement Design**

The study separately classifies five nonexclusive **regulatory approaches**:

- **Direct Limits**
- **User Choice and Control**
- **Parental Controls**
- **Warnings and Interruptions**
- **Broader Safety Duties**

Target population and operative status are coded independently so that the population protected, the regulatory mechanism selected, and the legal status of an intervention remain analytically distinct.

## Key Results

- **27 of 34 regulatory actions (79.4%)** address Engagement Design.
- **29 of 34 actions (85.3%)** primarily target minors.
- Among the 29 youth-focused actions, **13 (44.8%)** impose an account age restriction.
- **Parental Controls** appear in 18 of 27 U.S. actions (66.7%) and three of seven actions outside the United States (42.9%).
- **Broader Safety Duties** appear in six of 27 U.S. actions (22.2%) and six of seven actions outside the United States (85.7%).
- At the study cutoff, the 27 U.S. actions were classified as **10 In Force, four Partially Operative, five Not Yet Effective, and eight Blocked**. All seven qualifying actions outside the United States were In Force.

The broader pattern is **convergence on shared regulatory targets without a common legal model**. Governments increasingly regulate platform design and operation through different combinations of legal approaches.

## Scope and Interpretation

The study covers qualifying regulatory actions enacted or formally adopted between **January 1, 2018 and September 11, 2026**, with operative status assessed as of September 11, 2026.

The eight regulatory systems form a purposive comparative sample rather than a globally representative census. The analysis is descriptive rather than inferential and does not measure enforcement intensity, platform compliance, implementation costs, behavioral effects, or policy effectiveness.

Binary coding identifies the presence of qualifying legal requirements under the project's defined coding rules. It should not be interpreted as a measure of regulatory strength, quality, restrictiveness, or effectiveness.

For the complete research design and limitations, see the **[methodology](docs/methodology.md)** and **[project brief](docs/project-brief.md)**.
